Every scar is built differently

A scar is collagen laid down quickly to close a wound. Sunken scars form when too little collagen fills the space; raised scars form when too much does; pink, red or brown marks are left-over inflammation and pigment. Matching the treatment to the scar is what makes revision work, and most plans use more than one.

Your consultation

Mapping the scar

We look at your scar under good light, feel its texture and ask how it formed and how old it is. Scars should be fully healed, usually six months to a year, before revision. From there we build a plan with the order and spacing of treatments, how many sessions to expect and a realistic picture of improvement.

Results

Gradual, lasting change

Most scars improve 50 to 80 percent over a full course rather than disappearing. Collagen remodels for months after each session, so the best results show three to six months after your last treatment. Daily SPF protects the result, since healing skin darkens easily in the sun.
